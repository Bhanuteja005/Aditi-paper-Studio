# Aditi Paperie — vision, references and growth system

Research date: 16 October 2026. Brand name: **Aditi Paperie**, explicitly confirmed by the user.

## The business the transcript describes

Aditi Paperie is a handmade-paper studio with a commercial, educational and social purpose. The core is the material and the knowledge of making it; stationery, home objects, workshops and commissioned art are different ways that core reaches people. The strongest positioning is **“Handmade paper. Thoughtful objects. A craft to share.”** This is proposed positioning, not a quotation.

The founder describes textile offcuts and plant fibres becoming paper (00:20–02:19), paper becoming objects (09:13–11:26), livelihoods for women around Kanha (11:55–13:08), an academy that shares the craft (13:08–13:50), B2C/B2B ambitions and ongoing material research (14:19–15:01), and a platform that connects artists with hospitality buyers (15:35–16:33). The intention to help associated people grow is the emotional centre of the brand.

These are **client assertions and intentions from the supplied transcript**, not independently verified operating facts. The transcript says the venture began in March 2026 and that more than eleven paper types were being explored; neither establishes today's inventory or academy availability. `PROJECT_CONTEXT.md` is useful implementation history, but its categorical claims are not proof. Its old image prompts are not the creative brief for this redesign.

## Seven references worth studying

These are direct competitors or adjacent references selected for particular strengths, not a claim that any is the “best website.” Observations below come from accessible official page content and indexed page evidence; this is not a complete interactive usability or visual-performance audit of those sites.

## A system that can scale

| Audience / offer | First useful action | Minimum information | Operating record to create |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| B2C: paper, stationery, gifts, home objects | Explore a collection → view an item → buy when enabled, otherwise ask about that item | Actual photos, dimensions, material, price or honest price status, availability, care, delivery/returns | Product/variant, stock or made-to-order capacity, order and fulfilment |
| B2B: hospitality, architects, brands, corporate gifts | Request samples or discuss a brief | Application, quantity, dimensions, customisation, destination, deadline; publish MOQ/lead time only when established | Inquiry → qualified brief → sample → quotation → approval → production → dispatch |
| Workshops: visitors, families, groups, schools | View a confirmed session or request a group visit | Outcome, duration, age guidance, accessibility, capacity, location, inclusions, price and cancellation terms | Session, instructor, seats, participant, payment and attendance |
| Academy: learners and future practitioners | Register interest while being developed | Intended outcomes and level; clearly identify what is planned | Interest list → curriculum → pilot cohort → assessed learning → alumni/support |
| Artists and collaborators | Submit a portfolio or discuss a commission | Medium, proposal, relevant work, intended buyer/application | Artist profile and consent → collaboration agreement → artwork/provenance → commission |

The website can launch these journeys progressively. It should not pretend to have stock, open enrolment, automated booking or checkout when the operational service is not connected. “Request a visit” and “Register interest” are legitimate end states if the studio follows them up.

The reusable content foundation is **materials → products → makers → processes → sessions → inquiries**. Keep technical facts, availability and imagery in structured records so the same information stays consistent across pages. Add a real inquiry endpoint and delivery monitoring before relying on the contact form for business. Track completed requests by purpose; do not treat a button click or opened email client as a received lead.

## Claims that need evidence before publication

| Transcript/context claim | Evidence or clarification needed | Safer interim treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “100% tree-free” | Composition for each paper and all components of a finished object | Describe the confirmed fibre of the specific paper; avoid applying a paper claim to every product component. |
| “Chemical-free”, “100% toxic-free”, “non-toxic” | Process/ingredient inventory and relevant test evidence for the precise use | State the actual materials and process. Do not publish absolute safety claims. |
| Archival, centuries/thousand years, no yellowing, photo/document preservation | Relevant independent paper testing and suitability criteria; transcript wording around 04:45 is internally unclear | Omit preservation guarantees. Offer sample/specification information for specialist review. |
| Biodegradable, low-impact, water/carbon benefit | Defined conditions, coatings/inks/glues, measurements and methodology | Describe textile-offcut reuse where documented; no invented environmental counters. |
| Weather, insect or lamp safety | Product-specific testing and care/usage instructions | Describe indoor decorative use only where applicable; use verified lighting components and guidance. |
| Employment impact, paper-type count, founding date, academy status | Current studio records, founder confirmation and consent for personal stories | Attribute vision to the founder; distinguish a plan from an operating programme. |

The Constitution reference and sweeping claims about paper-industry pollution are unnecessary to communicate this brand and should not be used as evidence for Aditi's products.

## Logo research and original direction

- [**Kaagzi — Raksha Khandelwal, Behance**](https://www.behance.net/gallery/94850711/Kaagzi-Brand-Identity-Design-(12-)) is explicitly a hypothetical Indian paper-craft identity that also imagines an artist platform. It is a useful scope parallel, not an existing competitor or an identity to reuse.
- [**Paper Garden — Behance**](https://www.behance.net/gallery/241093663/Paper-Garden-Brand-Identity) describes a personal handmade-object identity built around natural textures. The useful question is how an identity behaves across paper, packaging and editorial material, not whether its logo can be adapted.
- [**Basalt Cove — Erin McDougle, Dribbble**](https://dribbble.com/shots/22829693-Basalt-Cove-Horizontal-Logo) provides a compact horizontal identity reference; it is for a spa, so category meaning must be reconsidered for Aditi.
- [**Arabella paper-goods identity pin, Pinterest**](https://in.pinterest.com/pin/brand-identity-i-designed-for-a-premium-paper-goods-company-arabella-logodesign-logodesigner-buylogo-branding--217017275776919763/) surfaced during research, but the full pin could not be retrieved. Treat it only as a discovery lead; creator attribution and details were not verified.
- [**Speck and Stone — Belinda Love Lee**](https://www.belindalovelee.com/speck-and-stone) is a stronger original-designer source: the case study explains carrying the product's texture into print with embossing and restrained organic marks. Translate the principle into paper-making; do not copy the mark.

**Proposed original concept: “The forming sheet.”** A simple deckled rectangular sheet and a restrained rising arc can suggest the mould, fibre settling into a surface, and a welcoming space for learning. Pair it with a highly legible Aditi Paperie wordmark. Keep one-colour ink as the master; gold is a secondary print finish. Test the symbol at favicon size, on a rubber stamp, on a paper label and as an embossed mark. A meditation reference can remain a separate founder-story detail unless the brand deliberately chooses it as its main category signal. This is a design recommendation, not a final approved logo or trademark clearance.

## Image direction

Use one coherent studio world: warm daylight, cotton white, pale earth, charcoal details, wood, stone and the muted colours of actual fibres. Alternate a wide working-studio scene, hand/process detail, tactile sheet close-up and carefully composed finished objects. Artisans should appear as capable adults concentrating on skilled work; avoid anonymous poverty imagery and stereotyped costume.

Generated images can establish an art direction, illustrate materials and present clearly labelled concepts. They must not masquerade as documentary portraits of real Aditi staff, the actual Kanha premises or accurate photographs of a purchasable item. Store provenance with each asset. Replace concept product images with a consistent real product shoot before commerce launches. This protects the exact quality that makes the studio persuasive: trust in the hands and material behind the object.
